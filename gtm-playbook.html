Part II

Six doors. Each one opens into the same room.

Every offering needs a different entry point because every audience lives in a different place. Parents don't find things where teens do. Youth pastors don't look where teachers look. These are the channels — proven or testable — and which offering goes through which door.

Channel 1 · Church networks

Leader Pack + Guide. Pitch to youth pastors via the Anglican DYC, Methodist youth councils, Catholic diocese youth offices. One denomination sold = 20–50 groups. Contact sheet already built for Penang; extend to AU churches. GTM action: Email the Penang 60 with a Leader Pack trial offer.

Channel 2 · Tutoring centres

Afterschool Agent. Pitch as a "whole-child add-on" for existing tutoring students. Parents already paying $50–80/hour for English/Maths; the Agent is a $44.88/wk upsell that teaches the skills schools and tutors miss. GTM action: Prospect list built, outreach template ready.

Channel 3 · Parents (direct)

Guide + Circles App. Reach parents via eSafety-adjacent content (the 2:07 AM page is the template). Social media posts, parent Facebook groups, school newsletter ads. The hook: "Your kid can navigate five apps but can't budget their allowance." GTM action: Run Essentials 1–4 as a social series, measure click-through to the hub.

Channel 4 · Schools

Leader Pack + Guide. Wellbeing coordinators and chaplains in Christian/independent schools. The eight essentials align with the Australian Curriculum's Personal and Social Capability. Price at school-budget level ($299 for a pack that serves 100+ students). GTM action: Build a one-page school prospectus from the Leader Pack.

Channel 5 · Creator partnerships

Afterschool Agent. AU Christian creators (Jamie Zhu, Timothy Yi) promote the programme to their audiences. Revenue share or flat fee per enrolled teen. Verified prospect list of 10 creators already built. GTM action: Send the 10-creator outreach list.

Channel 6 · Social media

Hub + Guide. Branded social cards for each essential, posted to Bluesky, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube. Not sales — education. Each post is "here's one essential, here's why it matters." The hub is the destination. GTM action: Essentials 1–8 queued as a weekly post series.

Channel priority. Not all six can run in month one. The GTM Engineer's first job is to pick the three with the shortest path to a paying customer. My read: church networks (Leader Pack has a clear buyer and a known pain point), tutoring centres (the B2B playbook is half-written), and social (lowest cost, fastest feedback). Schools and creators are phase-two bets — longer sales cycles, more research needed.

Part IV

The GTM Engineer. Not a marketer. Not a salesperson. A builder.

This is not a content manager role. This is the person who takes a finished offering and drives it into the world — through outreach, partnerships, social presence, and one-to-one conversations. They don't wait for leads to come in. They go find them.

Daily routine

The engine room

  • Check and respond to hub enquiries, emails, and social DMs within 2 hours
  • Run the morning social post (or queue it); engage with comments and shares
  • Send 2–5 personalised outreach emails (church, tutor, school, creator contacts)
  • Log every contact interaction in a simple CRM (Airtable / spreadsheet)
  • Review the Afterschool Agent session recording or notes from the day before
  • Update the phase tracker: what's complete, what's blocked, what changed
Weekly rhythm

The big moves

  • Write and schedule 1 parent-oriented long-form post for the week
  • Check Afterschool Agent enrolment status; follow up with leads who asked but haven't enrolled
  • Prepare and send the weekly email to active church groups (encouragement + tip)
  • Run the channel prioritisation: which channel got attention this week, what was the result
  • Update the Leader Pack or Guide based on feedback from active users
  • Review analytics: hub traffic, social engagement, email open rates
Monthly review

The scoreboard

  • Revenue by offering (Guide subs, Agent fees, Leader Pack sales, App donations if any)
  • New contacts by channel (church / tutor / school / creator / social / parent)
  • Conversion rate: contact → conversation → trial → paid
  • Afterschool Agent retention: how many teens completed the programme
  • Phase progress: on track, ahead, or behind for the current phase
  • Decide: which channels to keep, drop, or test next month
Core deliverables

What the GTM Engineer actually produces

  • 100 outreach contacts logged and followed up per 30 days
  • 20+ social posts published across all platforms per month
  • 2–5 active church / tutor / school conversations at any time
  • Afterschool Agent cohorts hitting minimum enrolment (3+ teens)
  • Hub updated with every new page, asset, and case study
  • Monthly phase report with decisions for the next month
What this role is not. It is not a content farm — the GTM Engineer does not write all the essential posts from scratch (the brand system generates those). It is not a customer support role — the Agent, Guide, and App are designed to be self-serve. It is not a sales role with quotas — the goal is to build channels that become self-sustaining over the five months, not to hit revenue targets by pressuring contacts. The GTM Engineer's job is to test every channel rigorously, eliminate the ones that don't work, and document what does so that by January the next iteration knows exactly where to focus.
Part V

What this plan doesn't know yet.

A go-to-market strategy is a bet on assumptions. These are the assumptions that need to be stress-tested before the plan becomes a forecast. If any of them is wrong, the plan adapts — but only if we know which one broke.

Assumption 1

Church groups will pay $299 for a Leader Pack. Test: offer it for $99 to the first 5 groups. If they say no at $99, the product is wrong or the pitch is wrong.

Assumption 2

Teens will stay in a 4-week programme with 3 live sessions a week. Test: track attendance per session in cohort 1. If attendance drops below 50% by week 3, the format needs to change.

Assumption 3

Parents will pay for an "after-school character programme" but not for "character formation." Test: A/B test the Afterschool Agent landing page with both framings. Measure conversion rate.

Assumption 4

Tutoring centres want a whole-child add-on. Test: if 0 of the top 20 centres respond after 2 outreach attempts, this channel is dead until a referral opens a door.

Assumption 5

The Guide subscription ($8.88/mo) will convert from free content. Test: measure hub-to-Guide conversion. If <1% of hub visitors subscribe, the pricing or the funnel needs work.

Assumption 6

Creator partnerships are worth the effort. Test: if 0 of the 10 creators respond to outreach, creator channel is deprioritised in favour of direct-to-parent content.

Honest note on this plan. The numbers, timelines, and conversion targets above are hypothetical test thresholds, not forecasts or projections. They exist to give the GTM Engineer a clear "keep going" or "change course" signal at every phase. If the phase 1 target (3 church groups) hits zero, the Phase 1 did not fail — the assumption about church groups was wrong, and Phase 2 shifts weight to tutoring or social. The plan is not a promise. It is a hypothesis generator. Market figures cited (tutoring pricing, school budgets) are estimates drawn from public sources and personal knowledge of the AU education sector; they are not independently audited.
