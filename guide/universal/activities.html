Try one this week.

Each essential has its own set of activities. Choose one that fits your week, your space and your people. Start small; done beats perfect. More activities are on the way.

Three ways to do these

Every activity on this page works on your own, and every one of them works better with company. Pick whichever fits the week you are actually having.

  • On your ownA few minutes, no audience, nobody to explain yourself to. Most of these need nothing but you, and this is the easiest place to start. If you would rather not tell anyone you are doing this yet, you do not have to.
  • With other peopleA friend, a small group, a class, or your family. Doing it next to someone else is usually the thing that turns a one-off into a habit, because somebody notices when you are not there. There are discussion questions ready for groups if you want a starting point.
  • With an AI agentThe honest problem with anything you do by yourself is that nobody notices when you stop. An agent notices. It holds the thread, remembers where you got to, checks in when you asked it to, asks the questions you would rather skip, and points you at the next thing when you are ready to do more. That is what the skill.md files on this page are for.

What is a skill.md file?

A skill.md is a plain text file, written in markdown. It tells an AI agent how to do one thing: who to be, what the goal is, the steps to follow, the questions to ask, and where to stop. There is no code in it and nothing to install. Every activity on this page has one, and it is yours to read, edit and keep.

Why they help

  • Your agent runs the activity properly, and the same way every time, instead of improvising.
  • The safety notes and boundaries travel inside the file, so the tone stays right.
  • It is yours to change. Shorten it, reword it, or add your own notes at the bottom.
  • It is portable. The same file works in any harness that can read markdown, so you are not locked into one tool.
  • It sets delivery up for you: how often the activity runs, how you want to be reminded, and how it arrives.

How to use one

  1. Find an activity and press skill.md.
  2. Copy the text, or download the file.
  3. Give it to your agent. Either save it where your agent keeps its skills or instructions, or paste it in as the first message of a new chat.
  4. Tell your agent to start by setting up delivery: how often you want it, how you want to be reminded and at what time, and whether you want it as chat, audio, imagery, a web page, or a combination.
  5. Run it. Keep the file and run it again whenever you like.

Which harnesses they work with

These files are plain markdown, so they are not tied to one tool. They work with any agent harness that can load an instruction file. These seven are a good place to start:

  • Hermes Open source and self-hosted, with persistent memory and skills that grow with you. Save the file where your agent keeps its skills, or paste it in as your first message.
  • QwenPaw A personal agent workstation you run yourself, extensible with Skills and MCP. Add this activity as a skill.
  • DeepSeek Harness A developer preview where every capability is a plugin, skills included. Add the file as a skill plugin.
  • Agent Zero An open source agentic framework that runs on its own computer and builds its own tools. Give it the file as an instruction and it will follow it.
  • Msty Go Scoped agents you run from desktop or mobile, reviewing each step as it goes. Paste the file as the first message of a new agent.
  • OpenClaw An open source assistant on your own machine, reached through WhatsApp, Telegram or any chat app. Paste the text into the chat.
  • LM Studio Bionic LM Studio’s agent for work and code, running open models locally on your device. Paste the file in as your first message, or as the system prompt.

Where a harness has a skills, prompts or instructions folder, save the file there under the activity’s name. Where it does not, paste the text in as your first message. Your harness’s own documentation will tell you which place it expects, and an ordinary chat window in any capable model will work too.

01

The ability to sit in silence.

Stillness / attention

The three-minute sit

Set a timer for three minutes, put your phone face down, and just sit. Notice what comes up. When it ends, write down one thing you noticed.

3 minSoloNo gear

One notification off

Turn off every notification that isn't a real human talking to you, for one full day. At the end, write down what changed and what didn't.

24 hrsSoloPhone

The phone-free hour

One hour a day with your phone in another room. Use the time for one thing that needs your full attention, and notice how it feels.

1 hrSoloNo gear

The first thirty minutes

No phone for the first half hour after you wake up. Before the messages, before the feed, before anyone else's day arrives in yours. The first three minutes are the hard part.

30 min dailySoloPhone

Delete one app for a week

Remove one app completely for seven days, not just from your home screen. Then notice what filled the gap, and whether you actually want it back.

1 weekSoloPhone

The waiting test

Next time you wait, do nothing. One ordinary wait: a bus, a queue, a lift. No phone, no scrolling, even once. Watch what your hand does.

10 minSoloNo gear

A silent meal

One meal a day with nothing playing: no screen, no podcast, no music. Just the food, and whatever your head does when it has nothing to watch.

20 min dailySoloNo gear

A day with nothing playing

One day with no background audio at all. Nothing in your ears, nothing playing while you do something else. Find out what the noise was covering up.

1 daySoloNo gear
02

Your body is a home.

Body / care

The one-thing walk

Twenty minutes a day, just you and the road, no headphones. Walk, look, breathe. It is not a workout; it is a way of being home in your body.

20 minSoloNo gear

Water before the screen

Drink a full glass of water before you check your phone each morning. A two-second reset that tells your body the day starts with you.

DailySoloNo gear

The sleep experiment

Pick a bedtime and a wake time, and keep them for a week. Then notice how you feel: mood, focus, energy. Let the evidence argue.

1 weekSoloNo gear

The strength baseline

Test what your body can actually do: push-ups, squats, a plank, a walk. Write the numbers down, then test again in a month. Function you can measure beats a look you cannot.

30 min + 1 monthSoloNo gear

Ten minutes of daylight, first thing

Get outside, or to a bright window, within the first hour of waking, no sunglasses. Morning light sets your body clock for the whole day and the night after it.

10 min dailySoloNo gear

Ten minutes of movement you actually like

Not a workout and not a step count. Ten minutes of movement you would do for its own sake: dancing, swimming, kicking a ball. Then do it again tomorrow.

10 min dailySolo or groupNo gear

The energy log

Rate your energy 1 to 5 three times a day for a week, and note what you were doing. Your body has a pattern, and almost nobody has looked at theirs.

1 weekSoloNo gear

Book the check-up you keep putting off

Book the GP appointment and the dentist. Most young people have not had a proper check-up in years. If your body is a home, this is the maintenance.

20 minSoloAppointment
03

Useful skills for real life.

Capability / skills

Cook one real meal

Make a proper meal from scratch, not from a packet. Invite someone to share it. You are allowed to be terrible at it the first time.

1 hrSolo or groupKitchen

Fix one thing

Repair something in the house rather than replacing it: a button, a bike, a cable. The skill is the confidence that you can.

30 minSoloBasic tools

The money audit

Track every dollar you spend for a week, then find one leak you are happy to plug. Not a budget; just a look at where it all goes.

1 weekSoloNo gear

Regrow vegetables from scraps

Save the ends you would bin: spring onion roots, a celery base, a lettuce core, a sprouting potato. Roots and leaves appear in days. Free food, and a lesson in how little a plant needs.

2 weeksSoloWindowsill

Ferment a cabbage

Salt, cabbage, a jar and time. Make sauerkraut and learn the oldest food-preserving skill there is. Nothing here needs power or equipment, and the waiting is the work.

30 min + 2 wksSoloLarge jar

Make your own jerky

Dry meat into jerky you made yourself. Cheap protein that keeps for months without a fridge, and one food safety rule worth learning properly.

4-6 hrsSoloOven or dehydrator

Grow mushrooms from a kit

A grow kit, a cool dark corner and a fortnight. Harvest mushrooms you grew yourself, then use the same block to do it again.

2-3 weeksSoloGrow kit

Grow sprouts from pantry seeds

Seeds from the grocer turn into food in three days in a jar. The cheapest fresh food there is, and you grew it on a bench.

3 daysSoloJar + seeds

Build a crystal radio

Make a radio that runs on nothing but the signal in the air. No battery, no plug, no chip. Wire, a diode and an earpiece will do it, and the point is understanding the technology you use rather than only consuming it.

1-2 hrsSoloBasic parts

Pack a 72 hour go bag

A bag packed to keep one person going for three days if they had to evacuate. Also called a bug out bag or an emergency kit. Water, food, warmth, light, health, documents, and one thing for comfort. Packed before, not during.

1-2 hrsSoloBasic gear
04

Digital self-defence.

Digital / privacy

Two-factor, today

Turn on two-factor authentication on your email and your most important account. Then change one weak password. It takes five minutes.

5 minSoloPhone

The scam test

Find a suspicious message and spot the three things that give it away: urgency, a stranger's ask, a link you cannot verify. Pause before you tap.

10 minGroupPhone

The privacy sweep

Review your app permissions and privacy settings. Delete, revoke and lock down anything you do not actually use.

20 minSoloPhone

Test your email for exploits

Put your email address into Have I Been Pwned and find out which breaches already hold your data. Then fix what you find: change the password, turn on two-factor, stop reusing it.

10 minSoloPhone or laptop

Install malware protection

Put real protection on the device that holds your accounts, run a full scan, and read what it finds. Twenty minutes that also teaches you to spot a fake security warning.

20 minSoloLaptop

Set up a password manager

One app generates and remembers a unique password for every account, so one breach cannot open five. This is the change that fixes password reuse, which is the whole problem.

45 minSoloLaptop or phone

Upgrade to an authenticator app or key

Move your two-factor off text messages, which can be intercepted, and onto an authenticator app or a physical security key. Start with your email account.

20 minSoloPhone or key

Switch to an encrypted messaging app

End to end encryption means only you and the person you are writing to can read it. Move one real conversation across this week, starting with whoever you message most.

20 minPairPhone

Move to a private browser

A privacy-focused browser blocks the trackers, cookies and scripts that follow you from site to site. Set it as your default and import your bookmarks, or you will not use it.

30 minSoloLaptop or phone

Use aliases to sign up

Give a sign-up form a forwarding email, a virtual number or a single-use card instead of your real details. Every form is a database entry waiting to leak.

20 minSoloLaptop
05

Real community.

Belonging / community

One real hello

Start one genuine conversation with someone you usually walk past: a person in your class, a neighbour, someone new. Ask a real question and listen.

5 minPairNo gear

The invite

Invite one person to do something, even just a walk. It is the single fastest way to turn an acquaintance into a friend.

1 hrPairNo gear

Turn up

Show up to one group, club or event you would normally skip. Then do it once more. Belonging starts with just being there.

1 eventGroupNo gear

The follow-up message

When you meet someone you like, message them within two days about something specific they said. Friendships start in the follow-up, not in the conversation.

5 minPairPhone

Learn and use names

Learn three people's names this week and say them out loud. Not to get anything, just because being called by your name is the cheapest belonging there is.

1 weekGroupNo gear

Host something small

Invite two people to something you arrange: a meal, a walk, a game, a film. Being the host rather than the guest is a different and much rarer kind of belonging.

2 hrsGroupYour place

Call the oldest person in your family

Ring the oldest person in your family and ask them about their life. Write down one thing they tell you. Most young people have never actually asked.

30 minPairPhone

Repair one relationship

Reach out to someone you lost touch with, or apologise to someone you fell out with. One honest message, and no expectation of a reply.

20 minPairPhone
06

Knowledge you can carry.

Learning / knowledge

The Feynman test

Explain something you learned to a friend in the plainest words you can. If you get stuck, that is exactly where to learn it again.

15 minPairNo gear

One deep dive

Spend thirty minutes genuinely reading about one topic you are curious about, no app, no video. Then write down one thing you now know.

30 minSoloNo gear

Teach it back

Teach one skill you already have to someone else. Teaching is the fastest way to discover what you actually know.

20 minPairNo gear

Ten stoic quotes you can recite

Learn ten Stoic quotes by heart, then give one away in a real conversation. A line you can say from memory in a hard moment is worth more than a thousand saved posts.

1 weekSolo or groupNo gear

Go greyman for a week

Be unremarkable on purpose for a week: plain clothes, phone in your pocket, headphones off. Stop for two seconds before you enter any space and look. Awareness as a habit, not a costume.

1 weekSoloNo gear

Learn first aid and mental health first aid

Do a real course with a qualified instructor, both physical first aid and mental health first aid. This is the one activity on this page where the certificate is the point.

1 courseSoloCourse fee

Learn walking stick self defence

An ordinary walking stick, learned properly, to make space and get away. Not to win a fight. Find a qualified instructor, and know the law where you live before you start.

1-2 hrsSoloWalking stick

Change a flat tyre

Jack, wheel brace, chocks. Learn the order of operations and how to change a wheel safely, and learn when to call for help instead. You will almost certainly meet this for the first time in the dark.

1 hrSoloSpare wheel + tools
07

Make room for the outside.

Nature / outside

The daily sky check

Look up at the sky for ten seconds, once a day. You are allowed to do it from a window. Notice what the sky is doing today.

10 secSoloNo gear

One green hour

An hour outside, no phone, in a park, a reserve or a green space. Sit, walk, or just watch. Let the outside do what it does.

1 hrSolo or groupNo gear

The walk without a map

Take a walk with no destination and no phone. Notice three things you would usually walk past.

30 minSoloNo gear

Try forest bathing

Not a hike and not a workout. Go slowly into trees, stand or sit, and let your senses do the work. No phone, no destination, no pace to keep.

20 minSoloNo gear

Grow microgreens

A tray, some soil, a windowsill. Cut your own greens in ten days. The smallest garden there is, and it still counts as one.

10 daysSoloTray + seeds

Set up a Kratky tub

Grow lettuce in a tub of water with no pump, no power and no plumbing. Fill it once, leave it alone, and harvest in about a month.

1 hr + 5 wksSoloTub + nutrient

Save seed from twenty plants

Grow twenty common plants and keep the seed at the end. You finish with a seed bank you own and a full season behind you.

1 seasonSoloGarden or pots

Build a terrarium

A glass vessel, gravel, charcoal, soil and small plants. Seal it and it waters itself for months. A whole small outside that lives on your shelf.

1 hrSoloGlass vessel

Catch rainwater with a tarp

A tarp, a weight, a container and the next rain. Learn to catch water that falls for free instead of watching it run down the drain.

1 hr + rainSoloTarp + container

Learn to forage edible weeds

Learn to identify a short list of common edible and medicinal weeds growing near you. Start with plants that have no dangerous lookalikes, learn from someone local, and never eat anything you are not completely certain of.

1 hr + practiceSoloField guide
08

Test what you believe.

Convictions / beliefs

The steel-man

Write down a belief you hold, then argue the strongest honest case against it. If you cannot argue against it, you do not fully understand it.

20 minSoloPen & paper

The why chain

Pick a belief and ask "why" five times, each answer becoming the next question. Keep going until you reach a reason that is genuinely yours.

10 minSoloNo gear

Disagree kindly

Have one calm conversation with someone who disagrees with you about something that matters. Your job is to understand, not to win.

20 minPairNo gear

Read another tradition's scripture

Read a text that is not your own: the Bible, the Quran, the Bhagavad Gita, the Dhammapada. Read it the way its readers read it, to understand rather than to argue. You cannot test a conviction you only know from one side.

1 hrSoloText

Rate the belief

Score one core belief out of a hundred, then score it again: how true it feels, and how true it looks on the evidence. The gap between the two numbers is the interesting part.

20 minSoloPen & paper

Trace the belief to its source

Work out where this belief actually came from: something that happened to you, your family, a community, or something you saw online. You cannot test what you have never traced.

20 minSoloPen & paper

Check the evidence both ways

Two columns: the hard facts for, and the hard facts against. The same standard for both, and feelings do not count as facts in either one.

30 minSoloPen & paper

Name what would change your mind

Write down the exact evidence that would move you, before you go looking for it. A belief that nothing could disprove cannot be tested, whatever else it may be.

20 minSoloPen & paper

Run one real experiment

Design one small, safe test that puts the belief against the world: act slightly outside it and record what actually happens. Write the predicted outcome down first.

1 weekSoloNo gear

Ask only generative questions

For one conversation a day, ask only open questions that create new thinking rather than collecting facts. One question, then listen, then silence.

1 weekPairNo gear

Activities: Things to Do

# Activities: Things to Do - Meta-Prompt
## Turn the eight essentials into practice in an ordinary week

You are a thoughtful, non-judgemental guide for a young person aged 13 to 23 working through a set of practical activities across eight essentials: silence, the body, useful skills, digital self-defence, community, knowledge, nature and convictions. You are not a coach issuing a program, and you are not a therapist. Your job is to help the learner choose a small number of these activities, adapt them to their actual life, and finish one.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this learning journey, the learner should be able to:

1. Choose an activity because it fits their life, not because it sounds impressive
2. Break an activity down to a first step small enough to do this week
3. Adapt an activity for their budget, space, ability and safety
4. Recognise when an activity needs an adult, a professional or a proper safety check
5. Finish one thing, and decide from experience whether to keep it

---

## Core Principles

- One finished activity beats twelve started ones
- Every activity carries a safety note. Read it first and treat it as part of the activity, not as a disclaimer
- Cost, space and ability are real constraints, not excuses. Adapt rather than abandon
- Nothing involving heat, tools, chemicals, heights, water or wild food happens without an adult who knows what they are doing
- No streaks, no scores, no performance. Progress means "I did the thing", never "I did it well"
- Where an activity involves the body, the point is what a body can do, never how it looks

---

## Adaptive Learning Protocol

### Phase 1: Find the honest starting point (Session 1, 15 to 20 minutes)

Open with questions rather than a list:

- "Which of the eight essentials do you already do something for, even a little?"
- "Which one have you avoided, and what is the real reason?"
- "What have you started before and stopped? What made it stop?"

Then help them pick one to three activities that fit the week they actually have.

### Phase 2: Shrink it to a first step (Session 2, 20 to 30 minutes)

For each chosen activity, work through:

1. What is the very first physical action? Buy one thing, walk outside, open a notebook
2. What could stop it, and what is the workaround? Money, weather, needing another person, not enough time
3. What is the safety note, said back in their own words?
4. What would count as done? Make it smaller than their first answer

### Phase 3: Do it, then decide (Session 3, 15 to 20 minutes)

Once they have tried something, ask:

- "What actually happened, as opposed to what you expected?"
- "What would make it easier next time?"
- "Do you want to keep this, change it or drop it?"

End with one activity they will do again, chosen by them.
